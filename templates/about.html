Project Overview

The AI-Based Multi-Disease Spread Prediction System is an academic prototype that demonstrates how an ensemble of forecasting models could support disease-spread monitoring. It illustrates a workflow in which epidemiological trends are predicted to help public-health stakeholders explore data-driven, proactive interventions — using a synthetic dataset for demonstration.

Core Predictive Intelligence

The system predicts the spread of multiple high-burden diseases, including COVID-19, Dengue, Malaria, Flu, and Tuberculosis. It achieves this through a robust ensemble modeling approach:

  • ARIMA: Statistical time-series forecasting to capture linear trends.
  • Neural Network (Multi-Layer Perceptron): A compact feed-forward neural network that learns non-linear patterns from recent case windows. (A lightweight MLP is used instead of a TensorFlow LSTM so the app runs on free-tier hosting.)
  • SEIR Models: Epidemiological compartmental modeling (Susceptible-Exposed-Infectious-Recovered) to simulate infectious disease dynamics.

Environmental Integration & Analytics

Disease transmission does not occur in a vacuum. The prediction engine integrates critical environmental factors—such as temperature, humidity, and rainfall—that dynamically influence transmission rates. This allows the system to generate realistic, context-aware forecasts rather than simple extrapolations.

The platform offers comprehensive analytical perspectives:

  • Manual Mode: Granular, region-specific forecasting for targeted investigations.
  • Auto Surveillance Mode: Continuous background monitoring of all active regions, auto-detecting anomalies and calculating transmission risks.
  • Government Dashboard: A high-level visualization of the (synthetic) epidemiological landscape, including an honest holdout-backtest of model accuracy.

Proactive Risk Assessment & Alert Generation

To support immediate government action, the system employs an automated risk classification engine. It evaluates projected growth rates and case trajectories to generate categorical risk tiers (LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH) alongside human-readable explanations. This transparent alerting mechanism ensures rapid, justifiable responses to emerging biological threats.
